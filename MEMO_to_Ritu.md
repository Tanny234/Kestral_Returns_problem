**To:** Ritu Deshpande  **From:** Jyotishka A Badiger  **Date:** 2 October 2026  **Re:** Returns model - what to do before dispatch

**The decision.** Do not hold flagged orders. Phone them before dispatch instead.
A hold only prevents a return when the customer gives up and cancels (about 1 in 8), and that happens to good orders as often as bad ones. Using Farhan's Rs 1,150 per return, holding the riskiest quarter of orders loses roughly Rs 60,000 a quarter. A Rs 45 confirmation call on those same orders makes money.

**The number.** I cannot honestly give you 95%. Only 11 in 100 orders come back, so a model that says "nothing will return" is already 88% right. The best model that uses only what we know at dispatch is about 90% right. A version showing 99% exists, but it reads the service system, which only fills in after the customer has already started a return - it would not work on a live order. What the model does well is ranking: among the 23% of orders it flags, about 1 in 3 really comes back (against 1 in 9 overall), and it catches 6 in 10 of all returns. It is wrong about 2 in 3 times it flags, so treat it as a call list, not a verdict. The board number should be stated as "returns caught and rupees saved", not accuracy.

**The rupees.** On the last three months of orders (about 2,100 orders, Rs 2.8 lakh of return cost): calling the flagged 23% saves about **Rs 36,000** net of call costs (range Rs 27,000 - 45,000), roughly Rs 1.4 lakh a year. Calling everyone saves about Rs 3,000. Holding costs about Rs 60,000 (assuming a 25% margin on the lost sales - Farhan please confirm the real margin). This trims the problem; it does not remove it. The spring pilot's 35% result drives these figures: if calls only prevent 20% of returns, the saving falls to about Rs 11,000.
